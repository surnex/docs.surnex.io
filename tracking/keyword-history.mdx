---
title: "Keyword history"
description: "Inspect one keyword's position over time, and which page ranked on each date."
---

Open a keyword's history by clicking its card on [Tracking](/tracking/add-keywords), a row in [Position changes](/tracking/changes), or any alert. **Back to tracking** returns you to the list.

The heading is the keyword itself; the subtitle shows the URL currently ranking for it.

## Date range

The picker in the header defaults to the **last 30 days**. Widen it to see long-term trend, narrow it to isolate a change you're investigating.

The range filters both the chart and the table below it.

## Position over time chart

A line chart of position by date, with the axis **inverted** — position 1 sits at the top, so an improving keyword climbs. Gaps in the line are dates with no check: a keyword tracked weekly plots roughly four points a month, and a paused period leaves a hole.

## History table

One row per check, with four columns:

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| **Date** | The date the check ran |
| **Position** | Your rank that day, or `—` if you didn't rank in the top 100 |
| **SE Results** | How many results the search engine reported for the query |
| **URL** | Which of your pages ranked on that date |

The **URL** column is the one worth reading carefully. If it changes between rows, Google switched which of your pages it considers most relevant — often the real explanation behind a position swing that looks inexplicable from the number alone. A drop from 4 to 19 that coincides with the URL changing from your product page to a blog post is a relevance problem, not a ranking problem.

**SE Results** is a rough competition signal. A large jump usually means the query's result set changed shape — a new SERP feature, or a broader interpretation of the query — which is worth knowing before you attribute a position change to something you did.

## Empty state

**No history data** — "Position data will appear after the keyword is checked" — appears when the keyword has never been checked, or when the selected range predates its first check. A keyword added today shows nothing until its first check completes, usually within about 20 minutes of adding it.

Widen the date range before concluding the data is missing.

## What isn't here

- SERP features (beyond AI Overview, shown on the [keyword card](/tracking/add-keywords))
- Competitor positions for the same keyword — see [Tracking competitors](/tracking/competitors)
- Export of a single keyword's history; the [Export](/tracking/add-keywords#export) button covers all tracked keywords at their current position, not history

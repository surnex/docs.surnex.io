Click a keyword on [Local SEO](/local/overview) to open it — its grid across the map, and its ranking history at your listing. It's the local equivalent of [keyword history](/tracking/keyword-history).

## The grid

A square of points around your listing, north at the top, with your listing ringed at the centre. Each point shows your position in Google Maps for someone searching from there:

- A number — your position at that point
- **>20** — you're not in the top 20 there

Paid Maps results are left out, so a position is where you rank among the organic listings.

Above the grid, three figures summarize the check:

| Figure | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Top 3** | The share of points where you're in the top 3 — "of the map" |
| **Avg position** | Your mean position, counting only the points where you're found |
| **Found** | How many of the grid's points show you at all |

Pick an earlier check from the date menu to see the grid as it was.

## A point

Click a point to see the top 3 businesses there, with their ratings, and a link to the same search on Google Maps from that spot. Your own listing is highlighted when it's among them.

The question at a weak point is usually who is above you. That's where to look before changing anything.

## Reading it

**Read the shape, not one number.** A business is typically strong near its listing and weaker toward the edges. Strong at the centre and absent a few points out means you win searches from people already nearby, and lose the rest of your area to competitors closer to them.

**Track Top 3 over time.** It's the figure that moves when your listing gets better or worse across the area. **Avg position** can improve simply because you've dropped out of the points where you ranked worst.

**Proximity dominates.** Maps rankings depend heavily on where the searcher is, which is why a grid exists at all. Your business profile — categories, hours, photos, reviews — matters more here than anything on your website.

**Volatility is normal.** Local results shift more than organic ones. Compare several checks before acting on one.

## The history table

Below the grid, a chart and a table of your listing's position at the centre of the grid — one row for **every check**. A check that didn't find you in the top 20 reads **>20**, and the chart draws it at 20, so the days you dropped out show instead of being skipped.

| Column | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Date** | When the check ran |
| **Position** | Your position in the pack at your listing, or **>20** if you weren't in it |
| **Title** | The business listing title as it appeared |
| **Type** | The kind of local result |
| **Review Response** | The share of reviews the listing has responded to |

**Title** is the listing title Google displayed. If it changes between rows and you didn't change anything, Google has reinterpreted the listing — worth checking your business profile.

**Review Response** reflects review activity on the listing. Reviews are a genuine local ranking factor, so a position trend that moves alongside it usually isn't coincidence.

## Empty grid

A keyword's first check starts when you add it, and fills in within a few minutes. Only the keywords you added are searched — the location's others keep their last check. After that it follows the [local SEO schedule](/local/overview#schedule) — weekly by default.

If a keyword shows **>20** at every point, either the query doesn't return Maps results for your kind of business, or you're genuinely outside the top 20 across the area. Try the phrasing your customers use, and check you picked the right [listing](/local/locations).

A [location without a listing](/local/locations#locations-without-a-listing) has no grid — its keywords are checked once for the whole area until you pick its listing.

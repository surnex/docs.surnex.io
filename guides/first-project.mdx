Setting up takes about ten minutes. Three of the decisions are hard to change later, so this guide flags them as they come up.

## 1. Account and organization

[Sign up](/authentication) and sign in. [Onboarding](/onboarding/overview) is one screen: your name, your company, and its website. It creates your organization — which holds your projects, team, and billing — and a [sample project](/onboarding/overview#the-sample-project) for your site, filled with made-up data so you can see every page working. Nothing in the sample was collected from your site.

A new organization starts on a 14-day trial of the **Growth** plan, with no card required. Projects and members are unlimited on every plan; tracked keywords and the other allowances are listed on [Plans](/billing/plans).

An agency running several clients should decide now whether each client gets its own organization. Separate organizations mean separate billing, separate teams, and hard data isolation — but also separate subscriptions. One organization with several projects is simpler and cheaper; separate organizations are right when clients need their own invoices or must not see each other's data.

## 2. Create the project

Click **Create your project** on the sample's banner, or **Add Project** on the Projects page. Three fields matter more than the rest.

**Domain.** `example.com` and `www.example.com` are different hosts to a search engine. Enter the one you actually want measured. A domain can only be tracked once per organization.

**Location and language.** These decide which market's results you're measured in — the first decision worth getting right. You can change them later in [Project settings](/projects/settings), but existing history keeps the locale it was collected under, so a change leaves you comparing two different markets across the boundary.

**Keywords.** At least one is required. Click **Suggest Keywords** and Surnex returns up to 20 the domain already ranks for, with volumes, all pre-selected. That's a reasonable start for an established site. For a new domain, expect nothing back and type your own.

**Competitors** are optional but worth adding now — up to 10. Adding them here puts them in the first rank check, so you have comparisons from the start.

## 3. The first run

**Creating a project starts collecting straight away.** Rankings arrive within minutes, because the first rank check is bought live. Backlinks, GEO, a site audit of up to 100 pages, web vitals, domain overview, tech stack, keyword research and one LLM benchmark follow, each appearing as its job finishes. A site audit on a large site takes longest.

[Local SEO](/local/overview) waits for you: it starts once you [add your Google Business listing](/local/locations) and keywords at it.

That first run spends allowances like the buttons would — about five research lookups, one AI brand audit, and the audit's pages. See [Usage and limits](/billing/usage).

## 4. Set your schedules

After the first run, four features re-collect on schedules you control, in [Project settings → Schedules](/projects/settings#schedules):

| Schedule | Default |
| --- | --- |
| **Rank tracking** | Every day at 00:00 UTC |
| **Backlinks** | Every day at 00:00 UTC |
| **AI visibility** ([GEO](/geo/overview)) | Once a week at 00:00 UTC |
| **Local SEO** | Once a week at 00:00 UTC |

Scheduled checks don't spend your monthly allowances — they're covered by what you hold — so the cadence is about how fresh you need the data, not about cost.

The **Timezone** is applied, so set the hour in your own zone. Cadences are real intervals from the last run, not calendar rules: "once a week" is seven days later, not a fixed weekday.

Worth changing on day one:

- **Backlinks** to weekly. Link profiles barely move day to day.
- **Rank tracking** to every 3 days if daily movement is noise to you. Most sites don't move enough daily to justify it.
- **Local SEO** paused, unless you have a Google Business listing to track.

## 5. First real work

Once the initial run has finished, in this order:

1. **[Audit issues](/audits/issues)** — the most actionable list you'll have on day one. Fix errors first, sitewide patterns before one-offs.
2. **[Domain overview → Top Keywords](/domains/overview)** — what you already rank for. Anything at positions 11–20 with real volume is close to page one and cheap to move.
3. **[Tracking comparison](/tracking/competitors)** — check the competitors you added actually appear. A domain dashed on most of your keywords isn't a search competitor, whatever it is commercially.
4. **[AI Overviews](/ai/google-ai-overviews)** — read the trigger rate. Near zero means AI search isn't affecting your market yet and you can ignore that section for now.

## 6. Notifications

Keep an eye on the trial. When it ends without a plan chosen, scheduled collection stops, and a missed rank check is a permanent gap — history can't be back-filled. **Settings → Billing** shows how many trial days are left; choosing a plan early keeps the rest of them. See [Manage your subscription](/billing/subscription).

The completion notifications — audit finished, snapshot finished — fire on every scheduled run. Useful while you're waiting for something specific, noise after that.

## What to expect after a week

You'll have position history worth reading, a second [audit](/audits/compare) to compare against your first, and enough backlink snapshots for the [trend chart](/backlinks/overview) to mean something.

Before then, most charts have one data point. That's not a fault — it's what accumulating history looks like.

**Trends → Trending Now** lists searches spiking right now. Unlike [Explorer](/trends/explore), you don't supply the keywords — Surnex returns what's rising.

## The table

| Column | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Keyword** | The trending search |
| **Volume** | Monthly search volume |
| **CPC** | Average cost per click |
| **Competition** | Advertiser competition |
| **Categories** | Topic categories the term belongs to |

A **Filter by keyword…** box narrows the list.

## Reading it

Most trending terms are news, entertainment, or sport — irrelevant to almost every project. The filter is the tool that makes the page usable: search your industry's vocabulary rather than scrolling.

**Categories** is the faster filter of the two. Scan for categories adjacent to your market and ignore the rest.

Then apply the same judgment you would to any keyword:

- **High volume, zero CPC** — a news spike. Traffic that arrives, converts at nothing, and leaves within days.
- **Meaningful CPC** — advertisers are bidding, which means commercial intent behind the spike. These are worth acting on.
- **Rising but low volume** — potentially an emerging term. Cheap to target now and expensive later, if it's real.

## Acting on a trend

Trending terms decay fast, so the practical question is whether you can publish while the interest lasts. A term spiking today is usually over within a week — and if a page takes three weeks to rank, you've missed it.

Two situations where it works:

- You have an existing page that already ranks for something adjacent and can be updated today.
- The term looks like the start of a durable shift rather than a news cycle. Check it in [Explorer](/trends/explore) — a genuine shift shows sustained growth, not a single spike.

## Live, not stored

This page fetches on demand and stores nothing. Refresh for current data; there's no history of what was trending yesterday.

Along with [Explorer](/trends/explore), it's one of only two live pages in Surnex — everything else reads stored data collected in the background. See [How data collection works](/projects/data-collection).

## Locale

Results are for one **Country** and **Language**, which start on the project's research market. To see another, click **Change Market** beside the page title, pick them, and click **Change Market** at the bottom of the panel. Trending searches are strongly regional, so the United States shows US trends regardless of where you are.
